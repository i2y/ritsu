Deadlines and reminders

Every stage:

  • It is due 3 business days after it starts, at the end of the day.
  • Its approvers are reminded 2 business days after it starts, at 09:00.
  • When it is late, it goes up to the head above, and its deadline is counted again from then; when it is late again, the ticket expires.
  • When nobody can answer it, it waits for an administrator to add an approver.

The stage “manager”, in place of those:

  • When nobody can answer it, it goes up to the head above; at the top of the organization, the stage is skipped.

The stage “counsel”, in place of those:

  • When nobody can answer it, it goes up to the head above; at the top of the organization, it waits for an administrator.

A ticket ends within 192 business days at the most (4 × 38 at its stages, 3 × 10 with the requester, 10 paused). ritsu ringi doc --longest shows the dates of the longest.

Rules of a ticket

  • A requester does not approve their own request: they are left out of the approvers of every stage, and so is the one who submitted it in their name.
  • An approver may send it back to the requester, or to an earlier stage that has answered, at most 3 times in all, withdrawals counted. After that, they approve or reject it.
  • Sent back to the requester, it may be submitted again until the end of the day 10 business days after; then it expires. Only the fields that change are given again.
  • The requester may withdraw it until the stage “counsel” answers. Withdrawn, it comes back to them, to be submitted again or dropped.
  • An approver may pause the ticket, 10 business days in all: its reminders and deadline stop, and move later by the days it was paused. When the days run out, it goes on by itself.
  • Approvers may correct “Risks legal notes” and “Reviewer”.
  • An approver may reject it: it ends rejected, and cannot be submitted again.
  • The team that keeps the request may add an approver to the stage a ticket is at, and hand one approver's part over to someone else. Neither moves a deadline.

Comments

  • A person may add a comment to what they do (submitting, approving, sending back, rejecting, withdrawing and the others), or write one on its own. One with no comment is the same as one with an empty comment.
  • Who may write one is decided by the gate shared/company.gate, as for the other operations.
  • A comment is up to 2000 characters. It is kept in the ticket's history, on the line of what the person did, with who wrote it and when, and is not changed after. Writing one on its own changes neither the state of the ticket nor its deadlines.
  • An approver may reject or send back a request without a reason, but a reason written tells the requester what to correct.

Proxies

  • A proxy for approving answers the stages of the person they stand in for, beside them, on the days the row says. Their answer counts once, in that person's seat. They may also pause and resume, correct the fields approvers correct, and retract an approval they gave.
  • A proxy for submitting submits requests in the name of the person they stand in for, and may withdraw, submit again and drop them as the requester does.
  • Whatever a proxy does is kept in the history with whose place they did it in.

The organization's data has no proxy for this request.

Books and flows
